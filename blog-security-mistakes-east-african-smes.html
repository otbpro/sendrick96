5 Security Mistakes I Keep Seeing in East African SMEs

Small teams do not need a complicated security program to reduce common risks. Start with the basics below, assign an owner to each, and review the controls regularly as your business changes.

1. Shared admin logins with weak passwords

Shared accounts make it difficult to know who changed what and prevent you from removing access cleanly when someone leaves. Give each person an individual account, use a password manager for unique passwords, enable multi-factor authentication, and remove old accounts promptly.

2. No tested backups

Set automatic backups for important business data and keep a protected copy separate from everyday accounts or devices. Test a restore periodically so you know the files are complete and your team knows how to recover them.

3. Delaying software and device updates

Unsupported software and overdue security updates leave known weaknesses unaddressed. Turn on automatic updates where appropriate, keep a list of business-critical systems, and replace software or devices that no longer receive security support.

4. Treating every message as trustworthy

Unexpected requests to change payment details, share a login code, or open an attachment deserve a second check. Agree on a separate way to verify urgent requests, train staff to report suspicious messages, and avoid sharing verification codes with anyone.

5. Leaving business payments and data exposed

Use reputable payment providers and avoid collecting or storing sensitive payment details yourself unless your business is equipped and authorized to do so. Limit access to customer records, use secure connections, and review who can issue refunds or change payout settings.

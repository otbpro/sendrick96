Adding Crypto and Mobile Money to Your Checkout Safely

Adding payment choices can reduce friction only when the checkout remains clear and each method is supported by a provider you trust. Use this checklist to evaluate the customer journey, operational fit, and security implications before launch.

1. Design a simple, trusted checkout first

Show the total, delivery costs, expected timing, and a concise order summary before the customer pays. Make support easy to reach and keep the number of steps short. Explain what happens next after payment, including how a customer can get help if confirmation is delayed.

2. Work with licensed, battle-tested providers

Check provider availability in your target market, settlement currency and timing, fees, dispute handling, support, and integration requirements. For crypto, use a specialist provider rather than building wallet custody or key management into your own application. Confirm applicable local legal, tax, and compliance obligations with qualified professionals.

3. Protect payment and customer data

Use HTTPS throughout, keep payment credentials with a provider designed to handle them, and collect only the customer details required to complete the order. Restrict staff access, keep software updated, and document how to respond to failed or suspicious transactions. Never store card security codes.

4. Test the full payment journey

Test successful, declined, cancelled, and delayed payments on mobile as well as desktop. Check that duplicate clicks do not create duplicate orders, that customers receive a clear confirmation, and that your team can reconcile provider settlements with orders and refunds.
